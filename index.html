In the weeks after the merger, the office’s vocabulary shrank to a single, superfluous phrase: “synergy.” It was uttered at every stand-up, every slide deck, every coffee refill. Marta, a pragmatic senior analyst, kept her eyes on the spreadsheets. She knew the promised efficiencies were ephemeral; the real cost was in human friction.

Her counterpart, a young associate named Theo, was meticulous to a fault. He rearranged the shared drive’s folders nightly, a ritual that did nothing but exacerbate the team’s mounting anxiety. One Thursday, the server crashed. The backup was corrupt. Theo’s carefully curated taxonomy vanished. In the silence, Marta didn’t gloat. She pulled out a single, dog-eared notebook—her own manual log, kept since day one.

She handed it to him. “Here. The real data.” Theo stared at the penciled columns, then at her. The ubiquitous panic dissolved into a quiet, precarious peace. He smiled, a small crack in his armor. “I’m sorry,” he said. It wasn’t synergy. It was just work, shared.
